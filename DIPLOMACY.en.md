# Diplomacy and Territory

[Player guide](README.en.md) · [中文细则](DIPLOMACY.md)

## Countries and factions

The Axis, Allies and Soviet Union are the three playable factions. National ownership remains separate from faction membership: two countries in the same faction do not become a single country. Political colours, borders and hover information follow current national control.

Before Barbarossa, if the Soviet Union attacks a neutral country, only those two countries enter a limited war. This does not automatically bring in the Allies. In other cases, an attacked neutral country joins the opposing major faction and may bring that faction into the war.

Equal-cost movement routes prefer to avoid neutral territory. Before a player move, attack or paradrop starts a war, a confirmation explains the affected countries and consequences. Cancelling spends no action. Moving into range and attacking use a combined confirmation when both transit and target countries are involved. Nuclear strikes also require confirmation.

## Four different territorial changes

- **Occupation:** entering an enemy city changes its control and transfers eligible dependent territory. It does not automatically relocate land units merely because the map changes colour. Aircraft still based at a captured hostile airfield are destroyed.
- **Cession:** a treaty permanently transfers selected cities and territory. The former owner's units withdraw to the nearest suitable remaining national territory; aircraft need suitable airfields and ships need legal sea locations. This is distinct from ordinary occupation.
- **Annexation:** all territory of the annexed country transfers to the recipient, and the annexed country's military units are disbanded.
- **Surrender:** capturing a hostile country's capital normally triggers annexation. Special scenario rules can replace this outcome, as in the Winter War.

Political colours and national borders update for all four changes. City facilities and construction projects transfer with their cities. These actions affect the war and cannot be undone through Undo Move.

## Dependent territory

Each non-city hex belongs to its nearest city or cities within the same national territory. If several cities are equally close, the hex depends on all of them. It transfers to an occupying country only when that country controls every associated city. Treaty changes update the national territory used by this system.

This is a strategic hex approximation, not a survey-accurate reconstruction of every historical border.

## Conditional Soviet events

**Partition of Poland:** after Germany forces Poland to surrender, the pact can transfer eastern Polish city regions to the Soviet Union. German units withdraw from the ceded area. The boundary uses the game's city-dependent territories.

**Winter War, November 1939:** the Soviet Union and Finland fight a bilateral war. Finland taking Leningrad forces a Soviet armistice: temporary occupations are returned and forces withdraw. The Soviets taking Helsinki instead force Finland to cede Viipuri and its dependent Karelian region. Finland remains independent rather than being annexed. Both sides withdraw under the treaty rules.

**Baltic ultimatum, June 1940:** the Soviets annex Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania if they are still independent and have not joined a major faction. Their territory transfers and military units disband. Monthly turns combine the ultimatum and subsequent annexation.

**Bessarabia, June 1940:** Romania cedes Chișinău and its dependent Bessarabian territory; Romanian forces withdraw to remaining national territory.

All these events require the relevant countries and diplomatic conditions to exist. They are not unconditional rewards, and old saves do not replay events whose dates have passed.

## Other campaign events

Italy can enter the Axis in June 1940, followed by Hungary and Romania in November. Barbarossa begins in June 1941 and mobilises up to ten Soviet units, depending on free deployment locations. US entry in December 1941 adds Allied funds and income, enables American equipment in Britain and deploys advance troops where conditions permit. D-Day in June 1944 deploys Allied troops only on available enemy-held landing sites.

Some historical dispatches are narrative only: the result of a battle still depends on what your units do. Starting in 1941, Axis units on Axis-controlled Soviet national territory suffer 12 strength damage each turn during December–February.

Victory is assessed against the major hostile factions. A limited-war victory is not automatically a campaign victory. The campaign does not automatically stop in 1945.
